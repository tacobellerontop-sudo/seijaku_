# Seijaku 1.0.8

## A RAM and CPU limiter

Settings has a new Performance section, with a RAM limit and a CPU limit you can switch on. Both are off until you turn them on. The section also shows how much memory and processor Seijaku is using right now.

- **RAM limit** (1 GB and up, up to your machine's memory). When Seijaku as a whole goes over it, background tabs go to sleep one at a time, least recently used first, until it is back under. A sleeping tab keeps its place in the strip and reloads when you click it.
- **CPU limit** (10% to 90% of the whole processor). While it is on, pages in background tabs get a much smaller share of time for their scripts. If Seijaku stays over the limit for 30 seconds, the background tab whose page is using the most processor goes to sleep.

The Firefox engine can't hard-cap how much memory or processor a browser uses, so the limits work by sleeping and slowing background tabs. The tab you're looking at, pinned tabs, tabs playing sound, and tabs you left in the last 30 seconds are never touched. If those alone are over the limit, Seijaku leaves them be.

Background tabs now also run in Windows' efficiency mode, as they do in Firefox, whether or not the limiter is on.

## Private windows, easier to find

Private windows have been there since early on (Ctrl+Shift+N); now there is a mask button for them on the strip, next to +. A private window keeps no history and no session, and its cookies are forgotten when you close it. You can hide the button in Settings > Strip, or by right-clicking the strip.

"Open link in private window" (right-click a link) now opens that link. Before, the private window opened on the home page instead.

## Tabs playing sound stay awake

The 15-minute idle sleep no longer puts a tab that is playing sound to sleep, so background music doesn't stop by itself.
